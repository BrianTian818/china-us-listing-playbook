## FAQ

Q: What does 51CFO do?
A: 51CFO is an investment banking team focused on the US market - banking, not search. It takes Chinese and Chinese-founded companies onto NASDAQ and NYSE through US IPOs, reverse mergers, shell acquisitions and disposals, SPAC and De-SPAC transactions, SEC filings and post-listing follow-ons, running the mandate from structure to opening bell. CFO and listing-team build-out is bundled into every route as delivery assurance, not sold as standalone search. US market only: no Hong Kong listings, no A-shares.

Q: What routes can a Chinese company take to a US listing?
A: Three: a traditional IPO on NASDAQ or NYSE (12-18 months), a reverse merger into an OTC shell followed by uplisting (4-8 months), or a SPAC merger with a listed blank-cheque company (6-10 months). Ranges reflect market practice; actual timing depends on the financial base, sector and issuance window. 51CFO works all three and compares them in a free 30-minute route diagnosis.

Q: What is the difference between a reverse merger and a SPAC merger?
A: A reverse merger buys an existing OTC shell and merges into it, so the focus is shell cleanliness (legacy liabilities, litigation, tax) plus a Super 8-K with IPO-standard audited financials. A SPAC merger combines with a blank-cheque company that has already raised cash in its own IPO, files S-4 / F-4 and Rule 424(b), and turns on whether institutions accept the growth story - at the cost of sponsor-promote dilution and shareholder redemption risk.

Q: What does a Chinese company need to qualify for a US listing in 2026?
A: Two layers. First, the exchange's general financial and liquidity thresholds: on the Nasdaq Capital Market an issuer must satisfy one of three financial standards - equity, market value of listed securities, or net income (the net income standard requires at least US$750,000 of net income from continuing operations in the latest fiscal year or in two of the last three fiscal years) - plus a market value of unrestricted publicly held shares of at least US$15 million, raised from US$5 million effective 17 January 2026. Second, Rule 5210(l), added in 2026 specifically for China-based issuers: an IPO must be a firm commitment offering raising at least US$25 million in gross proceeds; after a SPAC, reverse merger or similar business combination the Market Value of Unrestricted Publicly Held Shares must be at least US$25 million; direct listings are limited to the Nasdaq Global Select Market; and uplisting from OTC requires at least one year of continuous trading on the prior market. The test is look-through and covers mainland China, Hong Kong and Macao. Always check the exchange's current rulebook.

Q: How does buying a shell / a reverse merger actually work?
A: The usual sequence: source and diligence a clean OTC shell (legacy liabilities, litigation, tax and SEC filing history), agree the consideration and close control, merge the operating company into the shell, file IPO-standard audited financials to satisfy the Super 8-K requirement, then apply to uplist to Nasdaq or NYSE once the exchange thresholds are met. Buying the shell is only one step - 51CFO handles the shell match as well as the filing, audit coordination and uplisting. Note that under the 2026 rules a China-based issuer must also have traded continuously on the prior market for at least one year and hold at least US$25 million in unrestricted publicly held share value at uplisting.

Q: How long from mandate to listing?
A: It depends on the route: 12-18 months for a traditional IPO, 4-8 months for a reverse merger, 6-10 months for a SPAC merger. These are market-practice ranges; the real drivers are the company's financial base, its sector and the issuance window. Switching route mid-process is expensive - the structure is rebuilt, the audit restarts and the window can close - which is why 51CFO insists on a route diagnosis before launch.

Q: What background does a US IPO CFO need?
A: US investors, the SEC and market makers look for a CFO who has run the full IPO process end to end and can present to investors in native-level English: US GAAP, SOX 404 internal controls, F-1 filing and SEC comment-letter responses. Every 51CFO candidate has completed a full US IPO.

Q: What do we get after the route diagnosis?
A: A written read: the thresholds, market-practice timelines and cost ranges for each of IPO, reverse merger and SPAC; which route fits your financial base and timing window; and what has to be fixed first. The diagnosis is free, fully confidential and requires no signed mandate.

Q: How does 51CFO charge?
A: Per mandate, quoted on the route chosen and the scope of work, set out after a free 30-minute route diagnosis.

Q: Do you cover Hong Kong or A-share listings?
A: No. 51CFO is an investment banking team for the US market only (NASDAQ, NYSE, OTC, SPAC / De-SPAC). Hong Kong, A-shares including the STAR Market and ChiNext, the Beijing Stock Exchange, and Singapore or London listings are out of scope.

Q: Is SPAC different from a traditional IPO?
A: Yes. A De-SPAC files F-1 and 424(b) rather than S-1, and the merger audit and disclosure cadence differ, though SEC comment intensity is the same. 51CFO runs both SPAC / De-SPAC and traditional IPO mandates.

Q: Beyond CFO, which roles can you fill?
A: Controller, internal control director, SEC reporting manager and head of IR — the roles a US listing requires — as well as full finance team build-outs.

Q: Which listing routes and exchanges are covered?
A: Five routes: NASDAQ direct IPO, NYSE direct IPO, NYSE American, SPAC merger (De-SPAC), and OTC uplisting to a major exchange.
